==========================================================================================
1. SUMMARY
==========================================================================================

Overall: about 50% of minimal system functionality.

Most of the firmware is written, but very little of it has been tested on hardware.
The Teensy is connected to the Pi 5 over USB (/dev/teensy), but it is not mounted, and
the firmware on it is older than the current source. Everything added since 2026-10-19
has only been tested in software (on the Pi, with simulated inputs), not on the board.

"Minimal system functionality" means:
  1. The Teensy and the Pi exchange data reliably over USB.
  2. The ultrasonic sensors publish distances to ROS.
  3. The GPS publishes a position fix to ROS.
  4. The e-stop button stops the rover and the cable spool.
  5. The spool pays cable out as the rover drives forward and winds it back in when
     the rover reverses, matched to the rover's speed, with safety cut-outs.
  6. All of the above wired, mounted and started with the rover's normal bringup.

3. WHAT WAS DONE IN THE 2026-10-23 SESSION
==========================================================================================

- Code review of the whole package. The main finding was that the spool had no
  shutdown if the Pi stopped talking; that is now fixed.
- Pi watchdog: the Pi now sends a heartbeat 5 times a second. If the Teensy hears
  nothing for 0.75 s, it stops the spool and keeps it off until it is told to start
  again.
- E-stop on pin 2:
  - Wired normally-closed, so a pressed button or a broken wire both read as STOP.
  - A press latches. The stop clears only on a reset, and only once the button is up.
  - The Pi forwards the e-stop to the rover driver's software stop, so the drive
    motors stop too.
  - One reset (joystick B button) clears both the rover and the Teensy.
- Spool safety cut-outs. The spool may run only if:
  - it has been commanded on,
  - the Pi is alive,
  - the e-stop is clear,
  - the rover's speed reading is less than 0.3 s old.
- Spool speed model:
  - Spool speed = rover speed / current wound radius, plus 3% so any error leaves
    slack rather than pulling on the cable.
  - At the rover's 0.4 m/s cap that is about 43 rpm with a full spool, 57 rpm at
    half, and 96 rpm near the core.
  - It stops paying out with 3 m of cable left.
  - It will not wind in past a full spool.
  - It flags when the rover is faster than the motor can follow.
- The Pi now reports the Teensy as connected only while it is actually sending
  heartbeats, not just while the USB port is open.
- The Teensy now counts messages it had to drop and reports the count.

For later work, see section 0.

4. ASSUMPTIONS (replace when real values are known)
==========================================================================================

Measured from the spool's CAD model (spool_drum.stl):
  - Core diameter                       76.2 mm (3 in)
  - Flange diameter                     228.6 mm (9 in)
  - Width between flanges               154 mm (about 6 in)

Spool motor hardware (on hand, 2026-10-24):
  - Driver: TB6600 stepper driver. Step (PUL), direction (DIR) and enable (ENA)
    inputs, opto-isolated; 9-42 V motor supply; current and microstepping set by DIP
    switches. It is NOT a serial controller, so it needs 3 plain GPIO pins and no UART.
  - Motors (StepperOnline NEMA 17, 1.8 deg = 200 full steps/rev, with encoder):
      17HS19-2004D-E1K   2.0 A/phase, ~52 Ncm holding torque
      17HS15-1504D-E1K   1.5 A/phase, lower torque (~40-45 Ncm)
    Figures for the 17HS19 come from a StepperOnline eBay listing. No listing for the
    exact 17HS15 part was found. Check both against the real datasheets or labels.
  - Signal voltage (from gmostar, 2026-10-24):
      TB6600              3 V (3.3 V) logic, matches the Teensy
      17HS15-1504D-E1K    3 V (3.3 V)
      17HS19-2004D-E1K    5 V
  - Encoder (both motors, "E1K"): built into the motor (per Sharif). 1000 PPR optical
    incremental, = 4000 counts/rev after quadrature decoding. The 17HS19 listing says
    it has DIFFERENTIAL outputs (A+/A-/B+/B-). The TB6600 has no encoder input, so if
    the encoder is used, its wires go to the Teensy, and the Teensy does the stall and
    missed-step checking.
  - Speed: a stepper's speed comes straight from the step rate, so no speed control
    loop is needed. At 1/8 microstepping, 150 rpm is 4000 steps/s. The encoder confirms
    the motor really moved and keeps the cable count (spool_add_rotation()).

Assumed - need real values:
  - Cable outer diameter                6.0 mm (typical Cat6)
  - Cable on a full spool               100 m
  - Motor top speed at the spool        150 rpm (MAX_SPOOL_RAD_S in src/spool.cpp).
                                        Plausible for these steppers, but stepper
                                        torque drops with speed; check under load.
  - Gear ratio motor -> spool           unknown (1:1 assumed)
  - Which motor drives the spool        unknown (the 17HS19 has more torque)

6. CONCERNS AND RISKS
==========================================================================================

Safety
  1. The e-stop only stops the drive motors through software (Teensy -> USB -> Pi ->
     rover driver). If the Pi hangs, the drive motors do not stop. A second
     normally-closed contact on the button must cut motor power directly.
  2. Running out of cable: the spool stops paying out at 3 m left, but nothing stops
     the rover yet. If the rover keeps driving, it pulls on the cable's anchored end.
     The Pi must stop the rover when this happens (next step 5d).
  3. The Teensy's pins are 3.3 V only. Any 5 V signal can damage it. Check the
     signal voltage of the ultrasonics (when powered at 5 V) before wiring.
     Spool encoder: the 17HS15-1504D-E1K is 3.3 V, so it can wire straight to the
     Teensy. The 17HS19-2004D-E1K is 5 V. The encoder being built into the motor
     does not change that: if its signal wires go to Teensy pins, a 5 V high can
     damage them. With that motor, use a line receiver that has 3.3 V outputs (e.g.
     AM26LV32), or level-shift A+ and B+. This only doesn't matter if the encoder
     is not connected to the Teensy at all. OPEN: confirm which applies before
     wiring.
     TB6600 inputs: 3.3 V logic (gmostar, 2026-10-24), the same as the Teensy.
 15. Stepper on e-stop: disabling the TB6600 (ENA) removes holding torque, so cable
     tension can turn the spool backwards. Decide whether the spool should hold
     (stay enabled at zero speed) or go limp. The encoder will keep counting either
     way. Steppers also miss steps silently when overloaded; the encoder check
     (step 5b) is what catches it.
 14. BMS wiring: with two packs in series, the upper pack's B- sits at the lower
     pack's full voltage. Tying both BMS GND pins to the Teensy shorts the lower pack.
     Put a digital isolator (ADuM1201, ISO7721) on BOTH BMS links (see include/bms.h).

Spool accuracy
  4. The cable count resets to "full" whenever the Teensy restarts. After a restart
     partway through a run, every spool speed is wrong until the spool is reloaded.
  5. The radius model is an estimate based on assumed cable diameter and length.
     Without a tension sensor, errors build up over a long run. The 3% overspeed
     means errors leave slack rather than pulling on the cable, but a dancer arm or
     motor-current monitoring would make it self-correcting.
  6. The spool follows the rover's speed estimate (/odometry/filtered), so wheel slip
     shows up directly as spool speed error. The spool cannot run while localization
     is off (deliberately).
  7. Winding in: if the spool falls behind a reversing rover, slack builds up under
     the rover. The 3% overspeed also applies when winding in, but this needs
     testing.

System
  8. All five easily reachable serial ports are already used (2 BMS, GPS, 2
     ultrasonics). The spool no longer needs one (the TB6600 uses step/direction
     pins), but there is no spare serial port for future devices without soldering
     to the pads on the underside of the board.
  9. While the GPS is unplugged or silent, the Teensy pauses for about 25 ms every
     3 s to resend its settings. This must be removed, or the step pulses must come
     from a hardware timer (step 5a). Otherwise the stepper stalls for 25 ms at a
     time and can lose steps.
 10. The Pi's heartbeat rate and the Teensy's timeout are linked. If the heartbeat
     drops below about 4 per second, the spool will cut out even though nothing is
     wrong.
 11. The two ultrasonic sensors may interfere with each other if they face the same
     way. They have only been tested one at a time.
 12. The message format is defined twice (Teensy C++ and Pi Python). The firmware
     build checks message sizes, but nothing checks automatically that the two
     sides agree.
 13. Version control: the package is in the team repo, but work is lost if it is
     left uncommitted. Two people working in the same clone at once is not handled
     yet; work one at a time for now. Several people share this Pi and Claude Code account, so
     commit and add a section 0 entry at the end of every session.
